      Market Impact & Fills
      
              
      
                
      
                
      
                
      
                
      
              
      
              
      Market fills pay half the spread plus impact of coefficient × daily volatility × √(shares ÷ 20-day average volume). An order bigger than the volume limit fills over several days (0 = no limit). Limit orders pay commission only. Pessimistic needs a limit to trade through its price & assumes a bar hits a stop before a target.
      
              
      Carry & Margin
      
              
      
                
      
                
      
                
      
                
      
                
      
                
      
              
      
              
      Interest accrues on cash every calendar day (ACT/360); borrowed cash pays the cash rate plus the spread, shorts pay the borrow fee on their value. Below the maintenance margin, positions are cut at the close. A rate series (e.g. FRED’s DTB3 T-bill yield) can be added on the Data tab.
      
              
      Sizing & Limits
      
              
      
                
      
                
      
                
      
                
      
                
      
                
      
                
      
                
      
                
      
              
      
              
      Applied to the strategy’s target weights at the close they are decided, using only data up to then. Target volatility scales them so the last lookback days of returns of those weights would have had that volatility; Kelly uses the fraction × mean ÷ variance of the same returns. Both stay within Max Leverage. Then the drawdown rule (full size again once the drawdown halves), the per-instrument cap, the net & the gross limit. Every change is listed in the Constraint Log. Share orders aren’t resized.
      
              
      1 bps = 0.01%. Orders placed at a bar’s close fill on the next bar, so your strategy never trades on data it couldn’t have seen.

              Optional Tools
              
      
              Always shown: Parameter Sweep, Walk-Forward Optimization, Monte Carlo Resampling & In-Sample vs Out-of-Sample.
              
    
              

    
              
      
              Parameter Sweep
              Re-run your strategy for every combination of PARAMS values & score each run. Tune on older data, then check the result on data the tuning never saw.
              
      
              
        
              
          
              
            
              
            
              
            
              
            
              
            
              
            
              
          
              
          
              Use start:stop:step or a comma-separated list. Up to 400 runs.
              
          
              
          
              
        
              
        
              Pick a parameter & a list of values, then run the sweep.
              
      
              
    
              

    
              
      
              Walk-Forward Optimization
              Pick the best PARAMS on a training window, trade them on the next stretch the tuning never saw, then move both windows forward & repeat. The stitched out-of-sample result is what tuning would really have earned.
              
      
              
        
              
          
              
            
              
            
              
            
              
            
              
            
              
            
              
            
              
            
              Training Window
              
          
              
          
              
          
              
        
              
        
              Choose the parameter values to try, then run the walk-forward test.
              
      
              
    
              

    
              
      
              Monte Carlo Resampling
              Shuffle blocks of your strategy’s daily returns into hundreds of alternative histories. A wide spread means the result depends heavily on the order of good & bad days.
              
      
              
        
              
          
              
            
              
            
              
            
              
          
              
          
              
        
              
        
              Runs your current strategy, then resamples its daily returns.
              
      
              
    
              

    
              
      
              In-Sample vs Out-of-Sample
              Split one continuous backtest at a date & compare the parts. An edge that disappears after the split was probably fitted to the past.
              
      
              
        
              
          
              
            
              
            
              
          
              
          
              
        
              
        
              Runs your current strategy & splits the result.
              
      
              
    
              

    
              
      
              Sub-Period Consistency
              Cut the backtest into equal back-to-back periods & score each one. A real edge shows up in most periods; one lucky stretch carrying the whole result is a warning sign.
              
      
              
        
              
          
              
          
              
        
              
        
              Runs your current strategy & splits it into periods.
              
      
              
    
              

    
              
      
              Start/End Surfaces
              Every start date s & end date e with s < e: your strategy backtested from s to e gives S(s, e), the benchmark held over the same window gives B(s, e). Shows whether the result depends on when you happened to start & stop.
              
      
              
        
              
          
              
          
              
            
              
            
              
          
              
          
              Windows start & end on the first trading day of each month, quarter or year in your date range. One backtest per start date; monthly over 10 years is 120 backtests. Short windows make annualized statistics swing wildly, so they’re hidden by default.

              Risk Report

              Capacity
              How much money the strategy can run before its own trading eats the edge. Re-runs it at larger account sizes with square-root market impact & the participation limit, and finds the size where the Sharpe ratio drops too far.
              
      
              
        
              
          
              
          
              A list; k, M & B work. Uses your impact coefficient & participation limit (impact coefficient 1 if it is off).
              
          
              
          
              
        
              
        
              Needs instruments with trading volume.
              
      
              
    
              

    
              
      
              Options Overlay
              Write calls on the shares your strategy holds, buy puts under them, or both (a collar), and see what it does to returns & drawdowns. Also shows a delta-hedged option on the same instrument: whether selling volatility paid. Option prices are modelled with Black-Scholes, not market quotes.

              Cost Sensitivity
              Re-run the backtest at rising trading costs. Strategies that trade often can look great at zero cost & lose money at realistic ones; this shows where the edge runs out.
              
      
              
        
              
          
              
          
              Each level replaces your commission % & slippage; the flat fee per fill is kept. Use a list or start:stop:step.
              
          
              
        
              
        
              Runs your current strategy once per cost level.
              
      
              
    
              

    
              
      
              Random-Timing Test
              Shuffle the days your strategy was invested, keeping how often & how much, & pair them with the real price moves. If random timing scores as well as yours, the entries & exits aren’t adding skill.
              
      
              
        
              
          
              
            
              
            
              
          
              
          
              Scores are Sharpe ratios before costs, so they measure timing alone.
              
          
              
        
              
        
              Runs your current strategy, then shuffles its timing.
